# OSAR Field Leak-Testing Program

**Program:** UC Riverside, Center for Environmental Research and Technology (CE-CERT)
**Role:** R&D Engineer II — field project manager
**Funding:** CARB contract 23RD009
**Status:** Active, multi-year field research program

## The Problem

Alternative-fuel heavy-duty vehicles — CNG buses, hydrogen trucks — carry compressed and
cryogenic fuel systems where an undetected leak is both a safety hazard and an emissions
compliance failure. CARB needs real field data on how these systems perform and fail
in service, not just lab bench data, to inform regulation. That means testing happens
on live fleet vehicles, on partner agencies' own schedules, in the field — not in a
controlled lab environment.

## My Role

Field project manager and primary point of contact for the program day to day:

- Coordinate equipment readiness, field logistics, scheduling, and compliance
  documentation across concurrent field activities with external partners including
  Orange County Transportation Authority (OCTA), Riverside Transit Agency (RTA),
  Caltrans, and Sunbelt Rentals
- Handle compressed-gas systems directly in the field, including high-pressure hydrogen
  refueling on Nikola hydrogen trucks and CNG bus fuel systems — real equipment, real
  safety procedures, not simulated
- Author and maintain the standardized SOPs, QA gates, and corrective-action
  documentation the field team works against
- Direct and mentor field technicians, student engineers, and visiting engineers on
  program execution
- Translate field results into structured status reporting for faculty Principal
  Investigators, program leadership, and CARB

## Why This Is Harder Than It Looks

A university research program running field tests on a regulator's timeline, across
multiple external partner agencies each with their own fleet, schedule, and site access
rules, is a coordination problem before it's a technical one. Equipment has to be ready,
partner access has to be arranged, safety procedures have to be followed exactly, and the
compliance paper trail has to hold up — all while the actual test conditions (a partner
agency's real, in-service vehicle) are outside the program's direct control.

## Why This Matters Beyond CE-CERT

This is cross-functional program management with real regulatory stakes: coordinating
dependencies across engineering, safety, external-partner, and regulatory stakeholders,
where a missed dependency doesn't just slip a schedule, it risks a safety incident or a
compliance gap CARB has to answer for. That is the same discipline a complex, growing
program in any regulated or safety-critical industry runs on.
